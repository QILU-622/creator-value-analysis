Should a platform change which creators receive scarce traffic and incentives?

I translated a resource-allocation problem into an explicit ranking rule, compared it with the existing rule at the same top-4,200 pool size, and defined the controls required before any online rollout.

Decision

Run a controlled first test. Do not launch platform-wide.

The adjusted list improves synthetic retention and efficiency, but average cash support also rises. The result is a testable trade-off, not a free gain.

Evidence

Why the allocation rule deserves review

1. Supply loss

18,742 creators enter the synthetic funnel, but only 2,819 become stable, monetised suppliers.

2. Resource concentration

The top 20% receive 42.9% of exposure while producing 30.2% of revenue.

3. Actionable segments

High-exposure / low-monetisation and high-potential / low-incentive groups support different operating actions.

Method

Adjusted ranking rule

Adjusted priority score = 0.60 × existing-rule percentile + 0.20 × retention percentile + 0.15 × winsorised efficiency percentile + 0.05 × low-resource-occupancy percentile

The existing rule retains the largest weight to limit migration. Efficiency is winsorised at the 1st and 99th percentiles before ranking.

Evaluation discipline

Hold selection size constant

  1. Filter to creators who completed a first publication.
  2. Calculate percentile-based ranking signals.
  3. Select exactly 4,200 creators under each rule.
  4. Compare retention, efficiency, cost and list migration.
A larger selected pool could manufacture apparent uplift. The fixed top-N comparison tests allocation quality instead of pool expansion.
